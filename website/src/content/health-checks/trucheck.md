faqs:
  - question: Does TruCheck replace conventional cancer screening?
    answer: No. TruCheck is designed to be used alongside standard screening such as mammograms, colonoscopies, and cervical smears, not to replace them.
  - question: Do I need a doctor to approve the test?
    answer: Yes. A healthcare provider must approve and prescribe TruCheck for you.
  - question: Is TruCheck suitable if I already have symptoms?
    answer: No. If you suspect you may have cancer or have symptoms, you should seek urgent medical advice rather than screening.
  - question: Can TruCheck be added to another health check?
    answer: Yes. It can be added to another health check as an upgrade, or booked as part of the Cancer Health Check.
  - question: Who is eligible for TruCheck?
    answer: It is generally for adults aged 40 or over with no previous or ongoing cancer. It may be suitable for adults aged 35 to 40 with a strong family history or other risk factors.
  - question: What cancers can TruCheck detect?
    answer: It screens for 70 solid-organ cancers, including melanoma, prostate, lung, breast, gastrointestinal, liver, gynaecological, and head and neck cancers.
  - question: Are there limitations?
    answer: Yes. False positives and false negatives can occur. It cannot detect blood or lymphatic cancers because it looks for circulating tumour cells from solid organ malignancies.
