**| Private Health Screening Explained**

### **What is a Health Check?**

A health check is a structured medical assessment designed to evaluate your overall health, identify early signs of disease, and reduce future health risks. Often referred to as health screening or an annual medical, it focuses on prevention rather than treatment.

Private health checks are typically doctor-led, combining a clinical consultation with targeted tests such as blood work and cardiovascular assessment.

### **Why Are Health Checks Important?**

Many common conditions — including heart disease, diabetes, and high cholesterol — can develop without noticeable symptoms.

-   A preventative health check helps to:
    
-   Detect early signs of illness
    
-   Identify risk factors before symptoms appear
    
-   Establish a baseline for future comparison
    
-   Support long-term health and wellbeing
    

Early detection often allows for simpler, more effective treatment.

### **What Does a Health Check Involve?**

A comprehensive health check usually includes a combination of:

-   Medical consultation with a doctor
    
-   Blood pressure and cardiovascular and respiratory assessment
    
-   Blood tests (e.g. cholesterol, glucose, liver and kidney function)
    
-   Weight, BMI, and lifestyle review
    
-   Personalised health recommendations
    

Depending on your needs, additional tests such as ECGs, lung function tests or extended blood panels may be included.

### **Who Should Have a Health Check?**

Health checks are suitable for a wide range of individuals, including:

-   Adults wanting an **annual health check**
    
-   Professionals seeking an **executive health screening**
    
-   Individuals with a family history of medical conditions
    
-   Those monitoring ongoing health concerns
    
-   Anyone looking for reassurance about their health
    

Even if you feel well, a health check can provide **valuable insight into your future risk.**

### **How Often Should You Have a Health Check?**

For most people, a **yearly health check** is appropriate.

However, frequency may vary depending on:

-   Age
    
-   Medical history
    
-   Lifestyle factors
    
-   Existing risk conditions
    

A doctor can advise on the most suitable interval for you.
